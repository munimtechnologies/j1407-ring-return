# Scoop check, 2026-11-24 (before pre-registration)

Question: has anyone published a test of J1407b's bound-orbit periods (Mentel et al. 2018: 11-18
yr, next eclipse expected 2018-2024) with photometry from after 2018, or a second eclipse?

Searches (2026-11-24):
1. arXiv API, all:J1407 OR all:"V1400 Cen", newest first: the J1407 papers are Klaassen et al.
   2025 (arXiv:2512.13254, ALMA non-detection of the 2017 source; no photometry), van Dam &
   Kenworthy 2024 (arXiv:2404.07378, BeyonCE light-curve modelling), Barmentloo, Dik & Kenworthy
   2021 (arXiv:2106.15902, search for small transiting companions in 19 years of photometry; no
   period constraint on the ring companion), Kenworthy et al. 2020 (arXiv:1912.03314), Mentel et
   al. 2018 (arXiv:1810.05171), Rieder & Kenworthy 2016, Kenworthy et al. 2015.
2. Web: J1407b V1400 Cen second eclipse search period constraints 2023-2026: only the 2015-2021
   papers above.
3. Web: "J1407" ring eclipse ASAS-SN OR TESS "no eclipse" 2022-2024 bound: none after 2018.
4. Web: "V1400 Cen" 2025 OR 2026 photometry eclipse ring return: nothing relevant.
5. Web: J1407b ALMA free-floating Kenworthy unbound: Kenworthy et al. 2020 and Klaassen et al.
   2025 (RNAAS 9, 326); neither tests the orbital period with photometry.

Closest prior work: Mentel et al. 2018 (data to 2018; period 11-18 yr or > 22 yr; next eclipse
expected from spring 2018, most plausibly 2021-2024).
What is new here: the first test of the 2018-2026 predicted returns with ASAS-SN and TESS, under
rules fixed before the data are analysed.

pages, ATel/RNAAS).
